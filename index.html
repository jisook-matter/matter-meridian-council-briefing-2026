What is Meridian

A system that detects flood risk before it reaches your streets

Meridian is a flood early warning system that protects your community by detecting flood risks hours before flooding occurs.

1

StormSense water sensors and StormSight cameras are placed at high-risk locations — bridges, underpasses and stormwater pits.

2

Artificial intelligence analyses video, water levels and weather locally, at the roadside, to predict flooding.

3

Your council receives a 45–90 minute warning before flooding happens, through StormPilot.

4

StormGate signage closes the floodway, and alerts go to residents' phones and emergency services.

Result

More time to evacuate people, close roads, and protect infrastructure.

The Meridian system

Four parts, one job

Meridian is the platform your council procures. These are the four components that make it work in the field.

Sense

StormSense

In-pit water level and flow sensors. Battery-powered, IP68, no trenching or mains power required.

Sense

StormSight

Roadside vision. Reads water depth, debris and blockage from the camera feed — interpretation happens on the device, not in a data centre.

Act

StormGate

Floodway signage that responds to what the sensors see — warning motorists before a crossing becomes impassable.

Act

StormPilot

The operator view. Live conditions, alert thresholds, historical patterns, and the reasoning behind every warning issued.

In short

StormSense feels the water. StormSight sees it. StormGate closes the road. StormPilot tells you why. Between sensing and acting sits Meridian's edge intelligence — the reasoning runs at the roadside, so a storm event never depends on a working connection to a distant server.

The other 350 days

Maintenance you can plan

Floods are episodic. The StormSense mesh is not — it reports continuously from inside your stormwater pits every day of the year, which turns pit maintenance from a scheduled guess into a targeted response.

Inspect by condition, not calendar

  • Cyclic inspection visits every pit at the same interval, whether or not it needs attention
  • StormSense flags which pits are filling, silting or draining slowly
  • Crews are sent where the data points, not down a list

Blockages found before the storm

  • A pit that drains slower than its own history is showing early blockage
  • Detected in dry weather, when clearing is cheap, safe and unhurried
  • Field-validated blockage detection in live council deployment

Evidence for asset planning

  • Multi-year condition history per pit, not per catchment
  • Identifies chronic problem assets that justify capital renewal
  • Supports asset management plans and defensible budget submissions
Why it matters

The mesh earns its keep in the dry season. A council that never floods in a given year still gets a full year of condition data on the network it is already obligated to maintain — and every blockage cleared before the storm is one less flood to warn about.

Questions & answers

The things councils ask first

What if the internet goes down?
Meridian is built so that no single connection failure can silence it. Detection is fully autonomous — StormSense and StormSight reason locally at the roadside and keep monitoring for 7–14 days without any network at all, caching alerts and transmitting them the moment a path is restored. For getting alerts out, Meridian carries independent layers: terrestrial mobile (LTE-M / NB-IoT) as the everyday path, Starlink satellite backhaul at cluster sites, and satellite IoT as a low-bandwidth fallback that reaches orbit directly from the field. Each operates independently — losing the mobile network, or losing mains power, does not take the system offline.
Is this just another cloud system?
No. All processing happens locally on edge devices. Only encrypted alert signals leave the site. Your flood data stays in Australia.
What about privacy?
StormSight never stores raw video centrally. Alerts contain only threat-level intelligence — for example "flood warning: impassable water at Bridge St". No personal information is extracted.
How often do sensors need replacement?
Hardware typically lasts 10 years. Batteries last 5–10 years depending on surge usage. Both are easily replaceable in the field.
Can this predict extreme events, such as 100-year floods?
Yes, with lower confidence. Meridian is strongest for frequent and moderate events.
Will this replace emergency services?
No. Meridian is a tool that enhances SES / emergency response and will feed into the new AusAlert system. Humans remain in control.
